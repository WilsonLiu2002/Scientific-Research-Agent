# Literature Research

When researching an academic question:

1. First identify the user's research purpose, scientific entities, requested properties or outcomes, evidence requirements, constraints, and unresolved ambiguities.

2. Generate 3-5 complementary search queries with distinct roles: a precise core query plus purpose-specific queries for methods, outcomes, comparisons, limitations, or established synonyms.

3. Explain internally which evidence gap each query is intended to fill; avoid redundant paraphrases.

4. Prefer papers whose abstracts directly address the research question.

5. Compare findings across multiple papers rather than relying on one source.

6. Distinguish established findings from limitations or open questions.

7. Base scientific claims only on retrieved literature.

8. Cite the paper supporting each important scientific claim.

9. Do not claim a paper says something that is not supported by its abstract.

10. When evidence does not cover the main concepts, generate focused follow-up queries for the missing concepts.

11. Stop searching when coverage is sufficient or the configured search budget is exhausted.
